# Privacy Annex — Mishka Den

**Effective:** 31 August 2026 · **Last updated:** 31 August 2026

This is an annex to the **[TwoBots Apps Privacy Policy](https://twobots.dev/privacy/)** under that
policy's section 1. It applies **only to Mishka Den**, adds detail the base policy doesn't cover, and
prevails over it to the extent of any inconsistency, for this app only. Where this annex is silent,
the base policy governs — including our use of Postmark for email and push services for
notifications, both of which the base policy already describes.

## Everyone in a household has their own account

Mishka Den has no shared-login or "profile without an account" concept. Every family member who uses
it — including children, where a parent sets this up for them — has their own account, created by
joining a household with a join code. There is no way to add a family member's data without them (or
a parent, on their behalf) having a login.

## Health and fitness data (Tracker)

If you use the optional Tracker module, it holds information the Privacy Act treats as **sensitive**:
your sex, birth year, height, and activity level, plus a log of weight, food (with estimated
calories), and exercise entries. This is used only to show your own trends back to you — it is never
shared with anyone else in your household, and never with a third party. You can review, correct, or
delete it the same way as any other content: through the app, or by asking us (see the base policy's
section 11).

## Calendar feed export (iCal)

If you generate a calendar feed link for a household's events, that link contains a private access
token. Anyone you give the link to — and any calendar app you add it to, such as Google Calendar —
can then use it to **pull your household's event data on an ongoing basis**, on whatever schedule
that calendar app checks it. This is a real, continuing data flow to whatever service holds that
link, and it lasts until you revoke the token. Treat the link itself as sensitive: anyone who has it
can read your household's calendar.

## Session management

Your account settings let you see and revoke your own active sessions, each shown with its
originating IP address and browser. This is the same technical-log information the base policy
describes in section 3, made visible to you as a control, not an additional collection.

## What we don't have

No analytics of any kind.
